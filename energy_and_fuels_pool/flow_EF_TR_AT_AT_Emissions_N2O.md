# Transport emissions (N2O)

### Flow Description
<!-- MANUAL:FLOW_DESCRIPTION:START -->
EF.TR-AT.AT-Emissions-N2O is taken from UNFCCC Common Reporting Tables, Table 1 using the categories give in Table 13 by Schäppi et al. (2025)<!--cite:schappi_annexes_2025-->.

**How the numbers are derived.** N2O from fuel combustion is not measured but calculated in the national greenhouse gas inventory as fuel consumption (TJ, by fuel type and source category, from the national energy statistics) multiplied by emission factors (kg N2O per TJ), following the IPCC 2006 Guidelines ([IPCC 2006, Vol. 2, Ch. 3](https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/2006gl/pdf/2_Volume2/V2_3_Ch3_Mobile_Combustion.pdf)); Norway uses a mix of IPCC default and country-specific emission factors, documented in the National Inventory Document ([Miljødirektoratet, NID 2026](https://www.miljodirektoratet.no/publikasjoner/2026/mars-2026/greenhouse-gas-emissions-1990-2024-national-inventory-document/)). Activity data and implied emission factors are reported in CRT Table 1.A(a). The values (kt N2O) are converted to N with the factor 28/44. For road transport, the emission factors depend on vehicle type, fuel and emission control technology (Euro class), and are combined with vehicle-km in the road traffic model used for the inventory.

**Interpretation.** Road transport (1.A.3.b) accounts for about three quarters of the flow. N2O from road transport increased in the 1990s (0.19 kt in 1990, 0.28 kt in 2000) because early three-way catalytic converters produce N2O as a by-product, decreased in the early 2000s with improved catalysts, and has increased again to about 0.31 kt since 2010, likely due to the growth in diesel vehicles with SCR and oxidation catalysts ([EMEP/EEA Guidebook 2023, ch. 1.A.3.b.i–iv](https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/analysis/publications/emep-eea-guidebook-2023/part-b-sectoral-guidance-chapters/1-energy/1-a-combustion/1-a-3-b-i)). Domestic navigation, aviation and railways contribute 0.02–0.05 kt each.
