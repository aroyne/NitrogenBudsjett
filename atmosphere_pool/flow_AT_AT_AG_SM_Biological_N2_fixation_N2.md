# Biological N2 Fixation (Agricultural Soils)

<iframe src="../output_files/plots/AT_AT_AG_SM_Biological_N2_fixation_N2.html" width="100%" height="400px" frameborder="0" scrolling="no"></iframe>

### Description
<!-- MANUAL:FLOW_DESCRIPTION:START -->
Schäppi et al. (2025)<!--cite:schappi_annexes_2025--> advises using data from the EUROSTAT Gross nutrient balance. For Norway this dataset (updated September 2026) gives only 0.06–0.55 ktN per year, since it covers grain legumes and not clover in grassland. According to the EUROSTAT metadata, the BNF in this statistic is calculated based on the area of leguminous crops and fixation coefficients. The production of leguminous crops (peas, beans etc) in Norway is very low and we assume that agricultural BNF is for the most part determined by leguminous crops such as clover grown on pastures and in fodder production.

Bleken & Bakken (1997)<!--cite:bleken_nitrogen_1997--> based their estimate for BNF from the sale of clover seeds: a sale of about 145 t seeds was estimated to be used to plant 95 000 ha of grass/clover mixtures (655 ha/t seeds). Together with a rate of BNF of 80 kgN/ha on this area, they found a total of 7.6 ktN per year and summed up to 8 ktN to account for BNF from free-living organisms and other sources. The rate of 80 kgN/ha agrees relatively well with later studies of agricultural BNF in Norway, where average values between 10 and 100 kgN/ha have been found; the highest values in particularly productive areas were up to 260 kgN/ha Hansen et al. (2020)<!--cite:hansen_engbelgvekster_2020-->.

Yearly statistics of clover seed sales are not available, but according to NIBIO Totalkalkylen (NIBIO, 2025b), the area where grass/clover mixes may be sown for pasture and fodder production (fulldyrka eng) has remained constant to within about 3 % from 1995 up to today. We therefore assume a constant value of 12 ktN per year, the middle of the range of 8–16 ktN per year given by Bechmann et al. (2023), as cited in Frøseth et al. (2025); the lower end of the range corresponds to the estimate of Bleken & Bakken (1997). In Sweden (Moldan et al., 2025)<!--cite:moldan_where_2025--> the value was found to be 34 kT in 2015, which is more in line with the values found before 2000.

**How the numbers are derived**

- Constant 12 kt N per year (range 8–16 kt N), the range estimated by Bechmann et al. (2023) and cited in [Frøseth et al. 2025, NIBIO Rapport 11(124)](https://www.landbruksdirektoratet.no/nb/filarkiv/prosjektresultater/NIBIO-delrapport%201%20-%20Nitrogenkilder%20-%20og%20str%C3%B8mmer%20i%20jordbruket.pdf/_/attachment/inline/5986a77d-5097-4b6c-838f-27bc6ebad941:917ae8899116a72498026f7ab9a6ca50f03acc7c/NIBIO-delrapport%201%20-%20Nitrogenkilder%20-%20og%20str%C3%B8mmer%20i%20jordbruket.pdf); most of it is fixed by clover in grassland.
- The Eurostat gross nutrient balance gives 0.06–0.55 kt N per year for Norway, as it only includes grain legumes such as peas and beans.

**Interpretation**

- The flow is constant at 12 kt N per year, since there are no annual data on clover in Norwegian grassland; the area of fully cultivated grassland has varied by less than 3% since 1995 (NIBIO Totalkalkylen).
- Sweden, with about three times the agricultural area of Norway, has 34 kt N in 2015 (Moldan et al., 2025).
