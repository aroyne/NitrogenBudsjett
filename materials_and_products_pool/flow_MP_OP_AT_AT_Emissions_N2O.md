# Industrial Emissions (N2O)

### Flow Description
<!-- MANUAL:FLOW_DESCRIPTION:START -->
**MP.OP-AT.AT-Emissions-N2O** are taken from UNFCCC common reporting tables, Table 2 as advised by Schäppi et al. (2025)<!--cite:schappi_annexes_2025-->. Emissions are substantial, at least before 2009, and the main source of emissions is from nitric acid production.

**How the numbers are derived.** The flow is the N2O from industrial processes in the national greenhouse gas inventory, CRT Table 2(I): the sum of 2.B Chemical industry, 2.C Metal industry, 2.G Other product manufacture and use and 2.H Other (2.A Mineral industry and 2.D Non-energy products from fuels report no N2O). N2O from nitric acid production is reported by the plants; the methods are documented in the [Miljødirektoratet, NID 2026](https://www.miljodirektoratet.no/publikasjoner/2026/mars-2026/greenhouse-gas-emissions-1990-2024-national-inventory-document/). N2O is converted to N with the factor 28/44.

**Interpretation.** Chemical industry (2.B, nitric acid production) accounts for over 90% of the flow. Emissions were about 4.4 kt N (6.9 kt N2O) in 1990, fell from 4.3 kt N in 2005 to about 1.1 kt N in 2010 after N2O-reducing catalysts were installed in the nitric acid plants, and have been 0.5–0.8 kt N since. Category 2.G (mainly N2O used as anaesthetic and propellant) contributes about 0.1 kt N2O per year.
