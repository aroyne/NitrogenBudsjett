### Flow Description
<!-- MANUAL:FLOW_DESCRIPTION:START -->
We have used data for grass and fodder production from SSB table 13648 «Avling i jordbruket (1000 tonn) og avling per dekar (kg), etter ymse jordbruksvekstar (F) 2021 – 2024» and 05772 «Avling i jordbruket, etter ymse jordbruksvekstar (1 000 tonn) (F) (avslutta serie) 2000 – 2020». Values prior to 2000 are found in the SSB Jordbruksstatistikk (Table 2.1/Table 20). The jump in data between 1996 and 1997 may be due to methodological differences, but we have not been able to confirm this. The protein content of grass and fodder is known to be highly variable. We have assumed a protein content of 15 % based on 2025 analyses of 13 000 grass samples from all over Norway by Tine/NorFor (Bakken et al., 2026)<!--cite:bakken_nitrogen_2026-->, and a standard Jones factor for N in protein. As shown in Figure 3 in Volden & Schei (2025)<!--cite:volden_bedre_2025-->, there has been a slight decrease in the measured protein content of the second harvest of grass silage during 1990-2024, but not the first harvest. Due to the variability we choose to use a constant value. 

The protein content above is given per kg dry matter, so the harvest must also be in dry matter. SSB reports grass (eng til slått) as dry matter: from 2021 calculated with dry matter percentages from Tine's feed samples for each year and region, and before 2021 as all grass harvest converted via dry matter to hay. The green fodder and silage crops («Grønfôr- og silovekstar»: Italian and Westerwolds ryegrass, forage rape and kale, grain harvested green or for crimping, green fodder mixtures and, from 2000, root crops for fodder) have no such note. In SSB's survey, farmers report these crops as silage volume (m³), number of round bales or tonnes of fresh fodder ([SSB](https://www.ssb.no/innrapportering/potet-og-grovforavlinger)), and the resulting yields of 1 550–2 400 kg/daa are several times the normal yields of these crops, typically 400–700 FEm/daa (NIBIO, 2026)<!--cite:nibio_gjodslingshandbok_2026-->, or roughly 400–800 kg dry matter/daa assuming 0.9–1.0 FEm per kg dry matter (own calculation; grass silage harvested at heading has 0.87 FEm per kg dry matter ([Statens landbruksforvaltning, 2011](http://img7.custompublish.com/getfile.php/4299838.1897.zsnnizlkkwizmt/Omregningstabell+og+f%C3%B4rnormer+2011.pdf))). Read as dry matter, the SSB yields would correspond to about 1 400–2 600 FEm/daa, far above the highest normal yield of 850 FEm/daa. We have therefore assumed that the green fodder figures are fresh (ensiled) weight and converted them to dry matter. The dry matter content varies between crops and harvest methods: directly harvested ryegrass, forage rape and green fodder mixtures contain 9–17 % dry matter at harvest ([NLR](https://www.nlr.no/kunnskap/fagartikler/grovfor/sor/avlingspotensiale-i-seint-sadde-ettarige-forvekster)), round-bale silage is reckoned at 25 % and direct-cut silage at 20–24 % dry matter in the conversion table for stored fodder used for crop failure compensation ([Statens landbruksforvaltning, 2011](http://img7.custompublish.com/getfile.php/4299838.1897.zsnnizlkkwizmt/Omregningstabell+og+f%C3%B4rnormer+2011.pdf)), grain for crimping is threshed at 50–65 % dry matter ([Agropub](https://www.agropub.no/fagartikler/krossensilering-av-korn-1)), and fodder root crops contain 9–18 % dry matter ([Agropub](https://www.agropub.no/fagartikler/rotvekstar-til-for)). We use a dry matter fraction of 25 %, the value used for round bales, with a range of 15–40 % (PERT distribution). The conversion reduces the flow by about 19 ktN in 1990, when green fodder production was large, and by 2–4 ktN/year after 2010.

Hohmann-Marriott (2025)<!--cite:hohmann-marriott_nitrogen_2025--> used similar data sources but arrived at a smaller N flow (40-45 ktN) using a protein content of 8 % and N content in protein of 15 % (Table S2). 

In addition to harvested (slått) forage above, this flow includes grazing on agricultural land (innmark), which the harvest statistics do not cover. This is taken from Budsjettnemnda for jordbruket's Totalkalkylen "Eng, beite" series (Budsjettnemnda for jordbruket (2025)<!--cite:bfj_totalkalkylen_2025-->), 1000 FEm/year, calculated by BFJ as 200 FEm/daa on innmarksbeite plus 18 FEm/daa aftermath grazing on eng til slått, both scaled by the actual-vs-normal-year harvest ratio (Landbruksdirektoratet (2021)<!--cite:landbruksdirektoratet_forressurser_2021-->). Converted to N using the same 150 g protein/FEm assumption as the corresponding utmark-grazing flow (FS.OL-AG.MM-Grazing-Nmix).
